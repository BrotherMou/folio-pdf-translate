\section{Reduced-order approximation}
This document tests the translation of scientific prose while retaining mathematical symbols. The displacement field is represented by a linear combination of basis vectors. The reduced coordinates describe the contribution of each basis vector.
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{u}(t) \approx \mathbf{V}\mathbf{q}(t), \qquad
\mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r},\quad r\ll n.
\end{equation}
The full model contains a mass matrix, a damping matrix, and a stiffness matrix. The external force depends on time. Projection reduces the dimension of the equation without changing the definition of these physical quantities.
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{C}\dot{\mathbf{u}} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{f}(t).
\end{equation}
The reduced mass matrix is defined by $\mathbf{M}_r=\mathbf{V}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{V}$. A similar construction applies to the stiffness matrix. The superscripts and subscripts in this sentence must remain intact.
\section{Error measure}
We measure the relative error over the observation interval. A small value indicates agreement between the full and reduced solutions. The fraction, integral signs, norm symbols, and integration limits should be preserved.
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon = \sqrt{\frac{\int_0^T \|\mathbf{u}(t)-\mathbf{V}\mathbf{q}(t)\|_2^2\,\mathrm{d}t}
{\int_0^T \|\mathbf{u}(t)\|_2^2\,\mathrm{d}t}}.
\end{equation}

\section{Eigenvalue problem}
The natural frequencies are obtained from a generalized eigenvalue problem. Here the eigenvector describes a vibration mode. The index identifies the mode and the squared angular frequency is the eigenvalue.
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{K}\boldsymbol{\phi}_i=\omega_i^2\mathbf{M}\boldsymbol{\phi}_i,
\qquad \omega_i>0.
\end{equation}
For a two-dimensional example, the stiffness matrix is symmetric. Its off-diagonal entries are negative. Translation should preserve the matrix brackets, signs, and all four entries.
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{K}=\begin{bmatrix}2k&-k\\-k&k\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf{M}=\begin{bmatrix}m&0\\0&m\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
\section{Energy and verification}
The total mechanical energy combines kinetic and elastic contributions. In the absence of damping and external forces, this energy remains constant.
\begin{equation}
E(t)=\tfrac12\dot{\mathbf{u}}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{M}\dot{\mathbf{u}}
+\tfrac12\mathbf{u}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{K}\mathbf{u},\qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}E}{\mathrm{d}t}=0.
\end{equation}
